# Operator Angel Outreach

## Overview

Operator angels are the most underrated source of seed capital in 2025. Former employees of Stripe, Airbnb, Figma, OpenAI, Notion, Linear, Vercel, Plaid, Robinhood, and similar breakout companies now have liquidity from secondaries and IPOs and are writing $25-100K personal checks.

The money is often less valuable than what they bring:
- **Domain credibility** — Their name on your cap table is a signal to institutional VCs
- **Customer intros** — They know the exact people at the exact companies you are trying to sell to
- **Hiring pipelines** — Their network is your talent pool
- **Operational expertise** — They have solved the exact problems you are about to face

The meta-strategy: close 5-10 operator angels BEFORE approaching institutional VCs. Their participation signals that smart people with real domain knowledge believe in you — which makes the VC conversation much easier.

## Workflow 1: Finding the Right Operator Angels

### Where to Find Them

**LinkedIn (highest quality)**
Search: `"formerly at [Company]" + "angel investor"` or `"formerly at [Company]" + "startup advisor"`

Filter by:
- 2nd degree connections (so you have a warm path)
- Joined company 2015-2022 (likely vested and liquid)
- Left company in the last 1-3 years (fresh exit, active deploying capital)

**AngelList**
Search for investors by portfolio. Find the operator angels by looking at who invested in companies similar to yours at seed.

**Twitter/X**
Follow people who post about building at your target companies. Many operator angels are active on X and post about deals they are doing.

**Communities**
- On Deck Angels
- Lunchclub (filter for angels in your space)
- Cerebral Valley (AI-focused)
- Exec network communities for your vertical

## Workflow 2: Approaching Operator Angels

### The Right vs. Wrong Way to Reach Out

**Wrong way (cold LinkedIn DM):**
> "Hi [Name], I am building [Company] and would love to tell you about it. Would you have 30 minutes to chat?"

This gets ignored 95%+ of the time. They receive dozens of these.

**Right way (warm intro + specific hook):**
1. Get a mutual connection to send a specific intro
2. The intro mentions something specific about why THIS founder + THIS investor is a match
3. You follow up with a 3-sentence email that leads with the most interesting thing

**Right way (cold with extreme specificity):**
If you cannot get a warm intro, cold outreach works IF:
- You reference something specific they posted, built, or said
- You make the connection to your company genuinely relevant to their background
- You ask for one specific thing (a 20-minute call, not "to tell you about my company")

**Cold email template that works:**

```
Subject: [Company] — You helped build the infrastructure we are now disrupting

Hi [Name],

I am [Your name], building [Company] — [one sentence what it does].

I saw your post about [specific thing they wrote/built at Company X]. What we are building sits directly at the intersection of [their domain] and [your domain] — and I think your perspective from [specific role/project] would be genuinely valuable.

We are raising a $[amount] seed round and I am being selective about who I bring in. Given your background, I would love to include you if you are writing checks.

20 minutes this week?

[Your name]
```

## Workflow 3: What to Ask Operator Angels For

### The Ask Structure

Operator angel checks typically range from $25K to $150K. Your ask should be specific:

> "We are raising $1.5M on a $7M post-money SAFE. We are looking for a handful of operator angels to fill the round alongside our lead. Given your background in [domain], I think your involvement would be particularly valuable because [specific reason]. Would a $50-100K check make sense for you?"

**The specific reason matters.** Operator angels are not just writing a check — they want to feel like they have a unique angle on the deal. Name it:
- "You built the payments infrastructure at Stripe — we are selling into exactly the companies you worked with"
- "You led product at Figma during their growth phase — we are applying the same collaborative workflow model to [our category]"
- "You were at OpenAI before the GPT-4 launch — you understand the capability curve we are building on"

**STOPPING POINT 4 — What is the specific value this operator can bring beyond the check?**

1. **Customer introductions** — They know the exact buyers I need to reach
2. **Technical expertise** — They have deep knowledge of the technology I am building on
3. **Hiring pipeline** — Their network includes people I want to recruit
4. **Credibility signal** — Their name on my cap table validates my category thesis to VCs
5. **Operational wisdom** — They have solved the scaling problems I am about to face
6. **Multiple of the above** — I want to articulate the full value proposition

## Workflow 4: Sequencing Your Operator Angel Round

### Why to Close Operator Angels Before VCs

The sequence matters:

1. **Operator angels close fast** — No partnership vote, no investment committee. Often decides in 1-2 conversations.
2. **Their presence on your cap table moves VCs** — A cap table with 5 ex-Figma product leads and 3 ex-Stripe engineers says more than any pitch deck
3. **They fill your round while you are in VC conversations** — You arrive at the VC meeting with $400K committed, $1.1M remaining. That is a fundamentally different conversation than arriving with nothing.

### The Sequencing Plan

**Phase 1 (Weeks 1-2): Operator angels only**
Close 5-10 operator angels at your target check size. Goal: have 25-40% of your round committed before you take a VC meeting.

**Phase 2 (Weeks 3-4): VC first meetings**
Now you walk into every VC meeting with: "We have $[X] committed from [number] operators with backgrounds from [Companies]. We are raising $[total] at $[cap] and have $[remaining] left."

**Phase 3 (Weeks 5-6): Close**
VC term sheet + cascade. Fill remaining capacity from operator overflow and angel connections.

3. **Just a few strategic names** — I want 2-3 high-profile operators for credibility, rest from funds
4. **Not sure — help me decide what mix makes sense for my company**
