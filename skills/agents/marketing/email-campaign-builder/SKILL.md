## Workflow 1: Build a Welcome/Onboarding Sequence

### The Most Important Sequence You Will Ever Write

The welcome sequence is not a courtesy — it is the moment when subscriber expectations, trust, and future engagement are set. Subscribers are most engaged in the first 48 hours. Open rates on welcome emails average 50-60%, versus 20-25% for regular campaigns. If you do not capitalize on this window with a deliberate sequence, you are leaving your most valuable real estate blank.

### Step 1 — Define the Sequence Goal and Architecture

Before writing a single word, answer:
- What is the ONE action you want subscribers to take by the end of this sequence?
- What objections or questions will they have before taking that action?
- What proof, value, or education needs to happen first?

**Welcome Sequence Architectures by Business Type:**

| Business Type | Sequence Length | Primary Goal | Conversion CTA |
|---------------|----------------|--------------|----------------|
| SaaS / App | 5-7 emails over 10 days | Activate the product | Complete setup / upgrade to paid |
| E-commerce | 3-5 emails over 7 days | First purchase | Shop now with welcome discount |
| B2B / Consulting | 5-7 emails over 14 days | Book a call | Schedule a strategy session |
| Content / Newsletter | 3-4 emails over 5 days | Habit formation | Reply / click / share |
| Course / Community | 5-7 emails over 14 days | Enrollment / join | Buy the course / join the community |

### Step 2 — Map the Email-by-Email Sequence

The structure below is the baseline. Modify for your business type, but do not shorten it below 4 emails — one email is not a sequence.

```
=== WELCOME SEQUENCE STRUCTURE ===

EMAIL 1 — "The Welcome" (Send: Immediately)
SUBJECT LINE: You're in — here's what happens next
PURPOSE: Fulfill the promise that got them to subscribe. Deliver the lead
         magnet, confirm access, set expectations for what's coming.
CONTENT:
  - Warm welcome (2-3 sentences, personal tone)
  - Deliver the promised asset or access
  - Preview the next email ("Tomorrow I'm going to share...")
  - One soft CTA (follow on social, reply to this email)
LENGTH: Short. 150-250 words.

EMAIL 2 — "The Origin" (Send: Day 1)
SUBJECT LINE: Why I built [product/company] (the honest version)
PURPOSE: Humanize the brand. Tell the founder/company story through the
         lens of a problem the subscriber recognizes in themselves.
CONTENT:
  - The problem you personally experienced (make the subscriber the hero)
  - The moment you decided to do something about it
  - What you built and why
  - What this means for them (bridge to their situation)
  - CTA: Reply and tell me your biggest challenge with [topic]
LENGTH: Medium. 300-400 words.

EMAIL 3 — "The Value Proof" (Send: Day 3)
SUBJECT LINE: [Specific result] in [timeframe] — here's how
PURPOSE: Demonstrate expertise and results without pitching. Build
         credibility through a case study, lesson, or insight.
CONTENT:
  - One concrete, specific result or transformation
  - The mechanism that made it possible
  - The key insight (the thing they probably don't know yet)
  - Preview of what's coming in the sequence
  - CTA: Click to read the full case study / watch the video
LENGTH: Medium-Long. 350-500 words.

EMAIL 4 — "The Problem Reframe" (Send: Day 5)
SUBJECT LINE: The real reason [common problem] keeps happening
PURPOSE: Shift the subscriber's understanding of their problem.
         Position your solution as the only logical answer.
CONTENT:
  - Name the symptom they experience (show you understand them)
  - Reveal the root cause they probably haven't considered
  - Explain why common approaches fail
  - Hint at the better way (do not pitch yet)
  - CTA: Reply with "yes" if this resonates
LENGTH: Medium. 300-400 words.

EMAIL 5 — "The Solution Introduction" (Send: Day 7)
SUBJECT LINE: Here's what actually works (and why it's different)
PURPOSE: Introduce your product or service as the logical next step.
         First direct mention of the offer — but frame it as a tool,
         not a sales pitch.
CONTENT:
  - Bridge from Email 4's problem reframe to your solution
  - What your product/service does (feature → benefit → outcome)
  - Who it's specifically for (and who it's NOT for)
  - Social proof: 1-2 specific testimonials with real results
  - CTA: Learn more / Start free trial / Book a call
LENGTH: Medium-Long. 400-500 words.

EMAIL 6 — "The Objection Handler" (Send: Day 9)
SUBJECT LINE: "I'm interested, but..." (the 3 most common doubts)
PURPOSE: Address the top 3 objections that stop interested subscribers
         from converting. Use real language from real customers.
CONTENT:
  - "Here are the 3 things people tell me before they start..."
  - Objection 1 + honest, evidence-backed response
  - Objection 2 + honest, evidence-backed response
  - Objection 3 + honest, evidence-backed response
  - Soft CTA: Reply with any questions / Here's the link when ready
LENGTH: Medium. 350-450 words.

EMAIL 7 — "The Gentle Deadline" (Send: Day 12-14)
SUBJECT LINE: Last chance to [specific benefit] at [price/terms]
PURPOSE: Create urgency for subscribers who are interested but haven't
         acted. Not manufactured scarcity — a real reason to act now.
CONTENT:
  - Acknowledge they've been reading but haven't taken action
  - Remind them of the specific outcome/transformation
  - The real deadline or reason to act now (trial ending, cohort
    closing, price change, bonus expiring)
  - Simple, direct CTA with urgency
  - "If this isn't for you, no worries — I'll keep sending [value]"
LENGTH: Short. 200-300 words.
```

### Step 3 — Write Subject Lines That Get Opened

Subject lines determine whether everything else gets read. A great email with a weak subject line is invisible.

**The Five Subject Line Frameworks:**

| Framework | Formula | Example |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| **Curiosity Gap** | [Topic] + [Open loop] | "The email tactic I was embarrassed to share" |
| **Specificity** | [Number] + [Specific result] + [Timeframe] | "47% open rate in 30 days — what changed" |
| **Direct Benefit** | [Outcome] for [Audience] | "3 subject line formulas for SaaS founders" |
| **Question** | [Question they can't ignore] | "Are you sending to your whole list?" |
| **Reframe** | "The real reason..." / "Why [common belief] is wrong" | "The real reason your open rates are dropping" |

**Subject Line Rules:**
- 35-50 characters for mobile preview (6-9 words)
- Never use ALL CAPS, excessive punctuation, or spam trigger words (free, guarantee, click here, limited time)
- Test 2 subject lines per send — even a 5% open rate lift on 10,000 subscribers = 500 more readers
- Preview text (preheader) is your second subject line — always set it explicitly

### Step 4 — Configure Sequence Timing and Triggers

```
SEQUENCE SETTINGS:

Entry trigger: [Form submission / Opt-in / Lead magnet download / Purchase]
Send time: Tuesday-Thursday, 9-11am subscriber's local time
Pause conditions: Subscriber replies → pause sequence for 48h
                  Subscriber purchases → exit sequence, enter customer sequence
                  Subscriber clicks CTA → tag as "engaged," continue sequence

Exit conditions:
  - Subscriber completes the goal action → move to active customer sequence
  - Subscriber unsubscribes → remove immediately, do not retry
  - Subscriber does not open emails 3-5 → flag for re-engagement, reduce frequency

Naming convention: [Company]-WELCOME-[Version]-[Date]
```

## Workflow 2: Write a Promotional Campaign

### Email Campaigns That Actually Convert

A promotional campaign is not one email. One email is not a campaign — it is an announcement that most people will never see (average open rate: 22%) and that the people who do see it will not act on without repetition and different angles. A real promotional campaign is a 5-7 email arc with a build, a middle, and a close.

### Step 1 — Design the Campaign Arc

Every promotional campaign needs three phases: Anticipation, Offer, and Close.

```
=== PROMOTIONAL CAMPAIGN ARC ===

CAMPAIGN: [Name — e.g., "Spring Launch" / "Product X Release" / "Flash Sale"]
DURATION: [5-10 days for most campaigns; 2-3 days for flash sales]
OFFER: [What you are selling, at what price, with what terms]
DEADLINE: [Hard deadline — the real end of the offer]

--- PHASE 1: ANTICIPATION (Days -3 to -1 before launch) ---

TEASER EMAIL (Day -3)
Purpose: Build curiosity and prime the list for what's coming
Subject: "Something I've been working on for months..."
Content: Tease the problem you're solving. Do not reveal the offer yet.
         Ask: "Reply and tell me if this sounds familiar: [problem]"

ANNOUNCEMENT EMAIL (Day 0 — Launch Day)
Purpose: Make the offer clear, compelling, and urgent
Subject: "[Product Name] is now available — here's what it does"
Content:
  - Lead with the transformation, not the product
  - Features → Benefits → Outcomes framework (see table below)
  - Social proof (beta testers, early adopters, testimonials)
  - Clear price with context (not "it's $297" but "for the cost of
    one hour of consulting, you get...")
  - Hard deadline stated clearly
  - Primary CTA: [Buy Now / Enroll / Get Access]

--- PHASE 2: MIDDLE (Days 2-5) ---

CASE STUDY EMAIL (Day 2)
Purpose: Proof. Real results from real people.
Subject: "How [Customer Name] got [specific result] in [timeframe]"
Content: One detailed story. Before → Method → After. Quote extensively.
CTA: Same as launch email.

FAQ / OBJECTION EMAIL (Day 3)
Purpose: Handle every reason not to buy.
Subject: "Questions I've gotten about [Product Name]"
Content: List 5-7 real questions with real answers. Be honest about
         who this is NOT for — it increases trust and conversion.
CTA: Same as launch email.

VALUE STACK / WHAT'S INCLUDED EMAIL (Day 5)
Purpose: Make the offer feel undeniable by itemizing the value.
Subject: "Everything you get with [Product Name] (full breakdown)"
Content: List every component, the standalone value, and the total
         worth versus the actual price.
CTA: Urgency reminder — deadline is in [X] days.

--- PHASE 3: CLOSE (Days 7-10) ---

LAST CHANCE EMAIL (Day 8)
Purpose: Re-engage the fence-sitters with urgency.
Subject: "48 hours left — [specific outcome] closes [date]"
Content: Short. Remind them of the transformation. State the deadline.
         "If you're still on the fence, here's the one thing to remember..."
CTA: Direct link. No friction.

FINAL HOURS EMAIL (Day 10)
Purpose: Close the campaign for real.
Subject: "This closes in [X] hours"
Send: 4-8 hours before actual deadline
Content: 3 sentences maximum. "Tonight at midnight, [offer] closes.
         If you've been thinking about it, this is the moment.
         [CTA link]"
CTA: Single link. Nothing else.
```

### Step 2 — Write Each Email Using the SOS Framework

**SOS: Stakes → Offer → Stakes**

Every promotional email follows this structure: Open with what's at stake (the cost of not acting), present the offer as the solution, close by restating the stakes and making the CTA unavoidable.

**Feature → Benefit → Outcome Translation Table:**

| Feature (What It Is) | Benefit (What It Does) | Outcome (Why It Matters) |
|---------------------|----------------------|------------------------|
| 7-module video course | Step-by-step system | Launch your product in 30 days without guessing |
| 1:1 onboarding call | Expert setup help | Skip 6 months of trial and error |
| Private community access | Peer support and accountability | You're not doing this alone |
| Lifetime updates | Always current material | Your investment doesn't expire |
| 30-day money-back guarantee | Zero risk | Try it for a month — if it's not right, get a full refund |

### Step 3 — Set Campaign Segmentation Rules

Do not send every promotional email to your entire list. Apply these rules before every send:

```
CAMPAIGN SEND RULES:

Exclude from ALL sends:
  - Unsubscribed (automatic)
  - Purchased this product before
  - Active in a different promotional campaign (cool-off period)
  - Complained/marked as spam in last 90 days

Suppress from CLOSE emails (Days 8-10):
  - Anyone who has already purchased during this campaign
  - Anyone who opened 0 of the first 4 emails (they will never convert;
    sending more damages your sender score)

Priority send for LAUNCH email:
  - Engaged segment (opened in last 30 days) → send first
  - Semi-engaged (opened in last 90 days) → send 2 hours later
  - Cold segment → do not include in promotional sends; run re-engagement first
```

### Step 4 — Measure and Optimize in Real Time

| Metric | Healthy Range | Action if Below |
|--------|--------------|-----------------|
| Open rate (launch email) | 25-40% | Subject line failure — A/B test immediately |
| Click-through rate | 2-5% | Offer clarity or CTA placement issue |
| Click-to-open rate | 15-25% | Email copy issue — rewrite body for next send |
| Conversion rate | 0.5-3% | Offer, price, or trust gap — add social proof |
| Unsubscribe rate | <0.5% | Frequency or relevance problem — segment harder |
| Spam complaint rate | <0.1% | Critical — stop sending, investigate immediately |

1. **Write the full 7-email campaign** - Give me your offer, audience, and deadline and I'll write every email
2. **Write launch day email only** - The most important single email in any campaign
3. **Write the close sequence (last 2 emails)** - The hardest emails to write — where most campaigns lose revenue
4. **Build the subject line sequence** - All 7 subject lines with A/B variants and reasoning
5. **Design the segmentation and send schedule** - Complete ESP setup with suppression lists and timing

## Workflow 3: Design a Nurture Sequence

### Building the Long Game

A nurture sequence is for subscribers who are not ready to buy yet — but will be. The average B2B buying cycle is 6-12 months. The average consumer needs 5-8 touchpoints before purchasing. Most brands give up after 1-2 emails. The brands that win are the ones still in the inbox 6 months later, still providing value, still being helpful — so when the subscriber is finally ready, there is only one obvious choice.

### Step 1 — Define the Nurture Map

Before building the sequence, map the journey from "not ready" to "ready to buy":

```
=== NURTURE SEQUENCE MAP ===

AUDIENCE: [Who this sequence is for — be specific]
TRIGGER: [What puts someone in this sequence — lead magnet, free trial,
          blog post opt-in, webinar registration]
GOAL: [What action defines "nurtured" — book a call, start free trial,
       make first purchase]
ESTIMATED JOURNEY: [How long does your typical buyer take to decide?]

--- SUBSCRIBER JOURNEY STAGES ---

AWARENESS (Weeks 1-2):
  Subscriber knows they have a problem but may not know your solution exists.
  Content goal: Educate on the problem. Make them feel understood.
  Frequency: 2 emails/week

CONSIDERATION (Weeks 3-6):
  Subscriber is actively evaluating options, including yours.
  Content goal: Differentiate. Show results. Build trust.
  Frequency: 1-2 emails/week

DECISION (Weeks 7+):
  Subscriber is ready to choose. They need a reason to act now.
  Content goal: Remove friction. Provide proof. Make the offer clear.
  Frequency: 1 email/week + triggered sends based on behavior
```

### Step 2 — Build the Content Architecture

The 70/30 rule governs nurture sequences: 70% value (education, insight, entertainment), 30% sales (offer, CTA, social proof). Flipping this ratio turns a nurture sequence into a spam sequence.

```
=== 12-EMAIL NURTURE SEQUENCE (OVER 8 WEEKS) ===

WEEK 1:
  Email 1: "The One Thing Most [Audience] Get Wrong About [Topic]"
    Type: Reframe/education
    CTA: Soft (reply, read blog post)

  Email 2: "[Number] Signs You're Ready for [Solution]"
    Type: Self-qualification
    CTA: Soft (download checklist, take quiz)

WEEK 2:
  Email 3: "[Customer Story]: From [Problem] to [Result] in [Timeframe]"
    Type: Case study
    CTA: Medium (view full case study / book a call)

  Email 4: "What I wish I knew about [Topic] when I started"
    Type: Founder/expert insight
    CTA: Soft (reply with your situation)

WEEK 3:
  Email 5: "The [Topic] Mistake That's Costing You [Specific Cost]"
    Type: Problem amplification
    CTA: Medium (read the full breakdown)

  Email 6: "[Tool/Framework Name]: How We [Achieve Result] Every Time"
    Type: Methodology reveal
    CTA: Medium (download the framework)

WEEK 4:
  Email 7: "Quick question about your [goal/challenge]"
    Type: Engagement / survey
    CTA: Direct (reply with your answer)
    [Use replies to trigger behavior-based branching]

WEEK 5:
  Email 8: "[Trend/Data] + What It Means for [Audience]"
    Type: Industry insight
    CTA: Soft (forward to a colleague / share)

WEEK 6:
  Email 9: "How [Company/Person] Got [Specific Outcome]"
    Type: Second case study (different persona)
    CTA: Medium-hard (book a call / start trial)

WEEK 7:
  Email 10: "Every question I get about [Product] — answered"
    Type: Pre-sale FAQ
    CTA: Hard (book a call / buy now)

WEEK 8:
  Email 11: "Still thinking about [Goal]? Read this."
    Type: Final push / decision email
    CTA: Hard (clear offer with deadline or bonus)

  Email 12: "What happens if you do nothing about [Problem]"
    Type: Cost of inaction
    CTA: Hard (final CTA — or move to monthly newsletter)
```

### Step 3 — Design Behavior-Based Branching

A linear nurture sequence is better than nothing. A behavior-triggered sequence is far better.

| Behavior | What It Signals | Triggered Action |
|----------|----------------|-----------------|
| Clicks pricing page link | High purchase intent | Move to fast-track sequence with offer |
| Opens 5+ emails in a row | High engagement | Add to "hot leads" segment, increase CTA intensity |
| Clicks case study but doesn't convert | Interested, needs more proof | Send 2 more case studies, then offer |
| Does not open 3 consecutive emails | Disengagement | Reduce frequency to 1/week for 4 weeks, then re-engagement |
| Replies to any email | Active engagement | Personal reply from founder/team, add to priority list |
| Downloads a resource | Topic interest | Branch into topic-specific sequence |
| Visits product page 3+ times | Evaluation stage | Trigger "still thinking it over?" email within 24h |

### Step 4 — Maintain Consistency Without Burning Out

Nurture sequences fail in one of two ways: either the content is so generic that subscribers stop opening, or the team runs out of content ideas and abandons the sequence entirely.

**Content Pillars for Sustainable Nurture:**

```
PILLAR 1 — EDUCATION (40% of emails)
  Examples: How-to guides, frameworks, industry data analysis,
            myth-busting, step-by-step tutorials
  Formula: Problem → Method → Result

PILLAR 2 — SOCIAL PROOF (25% of emails)
  Examples: Customer case studies, testimonials, before/after,
            user-generated content, reviews, metrics
  Formula: Person → Challenge → Transformation → Specific Outcome

PILLAR 3 — THOUGHT LEADERSHIP (20% of emails)
  Examples: Contrarian takes, predictions, industry commentary,
            personal lessons, founder stories
  Formula: Common Belief → Why It's Wrong → What's Actually True

PILLAR 4 — OFFERS (15% of emails)
  Examples: Free trials, demos, consultations, product announcements,
            limited-time promotions
  Formula: Stakes → Offer → Stakes (the SOS framework)
```

## Workflow 4: Optimize Deliverability

### Deliverability Is Infrastructure, Not an Afterthought

Deliverability is the discipline of making sure your emails land in the inbox — not the spam folder, not the Promotions tab, not the void. A 90% deliverability rate on a list of 50,000 means 5,000 people never see your email. That is 5,000 missed touchpoints, every single send. Most teams discover their deliverability problem only when open rates collapse — and by then, the damage to sender reputation takes months to repair.

### Step 1 — Audit Authentication (The Non-Negotiable Foundation)

If you have not set up all three authentication protocols, you are not doing email marketing — you are doing spam. Gmail and Yahoo mandate these for bulk senders.

```
=== AUTHENTICATION CHECKLIST ===

[ ] SPF (Sender Policy Framework)
    What it does: Tells receiving servers which IPs are authorized
                  to send email on behalf of your domain.
    How to check: DNS lookup → TXT record for your domain
    Should contain: v=spf1 include:[your-esp.com] ~all
    Common failure: Multiple SPF records (only ONE is allowed)

[ ] DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail)
    What it does: Cryptographic signature proving the email wasn't
                  tampered with in transit.
    How to check: Your ESP generates DKIM keys — add to DNS as TXT record
    Key length: Use 2048-bit minimum (1024-bit is deprecated)
    Common failure: DNS propagation delay — test 48h after setup

[ ] DMARC (Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting, Conformance)
    What it does: Tells receiving servers what to do when SPF/DKIM fail.
    Start with: v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:reports@yourdomain.com
    Progress to: p=quarantine, then p=reject over 4-6 weeks
    Common failure: Setting p=reject before SPF/DKIM are perfect
                    (will reject your own legitimate emails)

[ ] Custom sending domain
    What it does: Emails sent from mail.yourdomain.com instead of
                  via.your-esp.com — critical for brand trust and
                  deliverability scores.
    Setup: Add CNAME records provided by your ESP
    Check: Email headers should show your domain, not the ESP's

[ ] BIMI (Brand Indicators for Message Identification)
    What it does: Shows your brand logo next to emails in Gmail, Apple Mail
    Requirement: DMARC at p=quarantine or p=reject minimum
    Optional but high trust signal — implement after the others are solid
```

### Step 2 — Implement List Hygiene Protocols

Your list is only as healthy as the worst email address on it. Hard bounces, spam traps, and role-based addresses (info@, admin@, noreply@) destroy sender reputation.

**90-Day Rolling Hygiene Cycle:**

| Action | Frequency | Why |
|--------|-----------|-----|
| Remove hard bounces | After every send | A hard bounce means the address doesn't exist — continuing to send is a major spam signal |
| Remove soft bounce repeaters | Monthly | 3+ consecutive soft bounces = remove |
| Suppress unsubscribes | Immediately, automatically | Legal requirement (CAN-SPAM, GDPR, CASL) |
| Remove spam complainers | After every send | Even one complaint per 1,000 sends is too many |
| Run list through email verification | Quarterly | Remove invalid, disposable, and role-based addresses |
| Sunset unengaged subscribers | Every 90 days | Anyone who has not opened in 90 days should enter a re-engagement sequence or be removed |

**Email Verification Tools:** ZeroBounce, NeverBounce, Kickbox, BriteVerify

**Healthy Benchmark Rates:**

```
Hard bounce rate:    < 0.5% per send (alert at 0.3%)
Soft bounce rate:    < 2% per send
Unsubscribe rate:    < 0.3% per send (alert at 0.5%)
Spam complaint rate: < 0.08% per send (Gmail threshold: 0.1% triggers filtering)
Open rate:          > 20% (below 15% = reputation damage accumulating)
```

### Step 3 — Warm Up New Domains and IPs

Never send at full volume from a new domain or IP. ISPs need to build a reputation history for you — which requires a consistent, gradual ramp.

```
=== IP/DOMAIN WARMING SCHEDULE ===

WEEK 1: 250 emails/day → Send to your most engaged subscribers only
WEEK 2: 500 emails/day → Engage: opened in last 30 days
WEEK 3: 1,000 emails/day → Engaged: opened in last 60 days
WEEK 4: 2,500 emails/day → Engaged: opened in last 90 days
WEEK 5: 5,000 emails/day → Semi-engaged list
WEEK 6: 10,000 emails/day → Full list (excluding cold/unengaged)
WEEK 7+: Scale to full volume

Rules during warmup:
  - Send at consistent times (irregular sending patterns look suspicious)
  - Monitor spam placement with tools like GlockApps or MXToolbox
  - Stop scaling if bounce rate exceeds 2% or complaint rate exceeds 0.1%
  - Do NOT send promotional campaigns during warmup — send content only
```

### Step 4 — Monitor Reputation and Inbox Placement

```
=== ONGOING MONITORING CHECKLIST ===

Weekly:
[ ] Check Google Postmaster Tools (postmaster.google.com)
    → Domain reputation: HIGH (green) is required
    → IP reputation: HIGH
    → Spam rate: < 0.08%

[ ] Check bounce and complaint rates in your ESP dashboard

Monthly:
[ ] Run seed list test through GlockApps or Mail Tester
    → Tests inbox placement across Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo, Apple Mail

Quarterly:

[ ] Run full list through email verification service
[ ] Review and remove all unengaged subscribers (90+ days no open)
[ ] Check your domain on blacklists: MXToolbox.com blacklist check
```

**STOPPING POINT 5 — Deliverability Optimization**

## Workflow 5: Segment Your List

### Segmentation Is a Revenue Multiplier, Not a Feature

Sending the same email to your entire list is the single most common email marketing mistake. It inflates unsubscribes, suppresses open rates, destroys relevance, and wastes your most engaged subscribers' attention on content they already know. Segmented campaigns generate 760% more revenue than non-segmented campaigns (Campaign Monitor, 2019). That is not an incremental improvement — it is a different business.

### Step 1 — Identify Which Segmentation Dimensions Matter for Your Business

Not all segmentation dimensions are equally valuable. Start with the one that most directly predicts purchase behavior for your specific business.

| Segmentation Type | What It Uses | Best For | Example |
|------------------|-------------|----------|---------|
| **Engagement-based** | Open and click behavior | All businesses — start here | Active (opened 30d) / Warm (30-90d) / Cold (90d+) |
| **Behavioral** | Pages visited, product viewed, links clicked | E-commerce, SaaS | Viewed pricing vs. visited blog vs. started trial |
| **Purchase history** | Past purchases, frequency, value | E-commerce, subscription | First-time buyer / Repeat / High-LTV / Lapsed |
| **Onboarding stage** | How far through setup/activation | SaaS, apps | Not activated / Activated / Power user |
| **Lead source** | Where they came from | Multi-channel businesses | Paid / Organic / Referral / Partnership |
| **Stated preference** | Survey answers, preference center | Content, B2B | Topic interest, role, company size, goal |
| **RFM scoring** | Recency, Frequency, Monetary value | E-commerce | Champions / Loyal / At risk / Lost |

### Step 2 — Build the Core Engagement Segments (Do This First)

Engagement segmentation is the prerequisite for everything else. If you do not have this in place, your deliverability will suffer no matter what else you do.

```
=== CORE ENGAGEMENT SEGMENTS ===

SEGMENT: Champions
  Definition: Opened AND clicked in the last 30 days
  Treatment: Full send frequency. Test bold ideas here first.
             These are your best subscribers.
  Send frequency: Up to 3-4 times/week

SEGMENT: Engaged
  Definition: Opened at least once in the last 60 days
  Treatment: Full send frequency. Standard campaigns.
  Send frequency: 2-3 times/week

SEGMENT: Warm
  Definition: Opened in the last 90 days, but not in last 60
  Treatment: Reduce frequency. Focus on highest-value content only.
             Trigger re-engagement nudge after 75 days.
  Send frequency: 1 time/week

SEGMENT: Cold
  Definition: No opens in 90-180 days
  Treatment: Run re-engagement sequence (see Workflow 6).
             Do NOT send promotional campaigns to this segment.
             Sending promos to cold subscribers tanks your sender score.
  Send frequency: Re-engagement sequence only

SEGMENT: Dormant
  Definition: No opens in 180+ days
  Treatment: Final re-engagement attempt. If no response, sunset permanently.
  Send frequency: 1-2 emails, then remove
```

### Step 3 — Build Behavioral Segments Based on Your Funnel

Beyond engagement, tag subscribers based on what they DO, not just what they open.

```
=== BEHAVIORAL TAGGING SYSTEM ===

Set up these tags/custom fields in your ESP:

INTEREST TAGS (based on links clicked):
  tag: interest:pricing         → clicked any pricing page link
  tag: interest:case-studies    → clicked any case study or testimonial
  tag: interest:feature:[name]  → clicked link about specific feature
  tag: interest:comparison      → clicked "vs." or comparison content

FUNNEL STAGE TAGS:
  tag: stage:aware              → in early education content
  tag: stage:considering        → visited pricing, clicked product links
  tag: stage:decision           → started trial, booked call, added to cart
  tag: stage:customer           → purchased
  tag: stage:churned            → cancelled or lapsed

PERSONA TAGS (from lead magnet or opt-in form):
  tag: role:[founder/marketer/designer/developer]
  tag: company-size:[solo/small/medium/enterprise]
  tag: goal:[specific goal from opt-in survey]

CONTENT PREFERENCE TAGS:
  tag: content:video            → consistently clicks video links
  tag: content:templates        → downloads templates/tools
  tag: content:deep-reads       → opens long-form content
```

### Step 4 — Design Segment-Specific Send Rules

```
=== SEND DECISION FRAMEWORK ===

Before every campaign, ask:

1. WHO should receive this?
   Define: [Segment name + tag logic]
   Exclude: [Who should NOT receive this — list explicitly]

2. IS this relevant to this segment?
   Test: "Would a subscriber in this segment find this
          immediately useful or interesting?"
   If no → do not send → create a separate version that is relevant

3. WHAT is the right frequency for this segment?
   Champions: Up to 3-4x/week
   Engaged: 2-3x/week
   Warm: 1x/week
   Cold: Re-engagement only — never promotional

4. WHAT is the right CTA intensity?
   Awareness stage: Soft CTA (read, reply, download)
   Consideration stage: Medium CTA (watch demo, read case study)
   Decision stage: Hard CTA (buy, book, start trial)
   Customer: Expansion CTA (upgrade, refer, review)
```

## Workflow 6: Write a Win-Back Campaign

### Re-Engage Before You Lose Them Forever

The moment a subscriber stops engaging is not the moment to give up — it is the moment to fight for the relationship. Re-engagement is dramatically cheaper than acquisition: you already have their email address, they already opted in, and they have some prior familiarity with your brand. A well-executed win-back campaign will typically recover 5-15% of cold subscribers. Done poorly or not at all, those subscribers sit on your list degrading your sender reputation while you pay for them every month.

The rule: start at 60 days of no engagement, not 6 months. By 6 months, the relationship is gone and your deliverability has already taken the hit.

### Step 1 — Define Your Win-Back Trigger and Tiers

Not all cold subscribers are the same. Someone who was engaged for two years and went cold three months ago is very different from someone who never opened a single email.

```
=== WIN-BACK TIERS ===

TIER 1 — SLIPPING (60-90 days no open)
  These were engaged. Something changed.
  Approach: Personal. Warm. "I noticed you haven't been around."
  Goal: Re-engage before they become truly cold.
  Sequence length: 3 emails over 7 days

TIER 2 — COLD (90-180 days no open)
  The relationship has cooled significantly.
  Approach: Direct value offer. Give them a reason to come back.
  Goal: Get one open, one click — reset the engagement clock.
  Sequence length: 4 emails over 14 days

TIER 3 — DORMANT (180+ days no open)
  Last resort. Most will not re-engage.
  Approach: Blunt honesty. "Should I keep emailing you?"
  Goal: Get a definitive response — they re-engage or they are removed.
  Sequence length: 2-3 emails over 7 days, then sunset permanently
```

### Step 2 — Write the Win-Back Sequence

```
=== WIN-BACK SEQUENCE TEMPLATE ===

--- TIER 2 WIN-BACK (4-email sequence) ---

EMAIL 1: "We miss you" (Day 0)
SUBJECT LINE OPTIONS:
  - "It's been a while, [First Name]"
  - "Are we still good?"
  - "[Company] → [First Name]: quick check-in"
CONTENT:
  Opening: Acknowledge the silence directly. Do not pretend it
            hasn't happened. "I noticed you haven't opened our
            emails in a few months, and I wanted to check in."
  Value reminder: Briefly remind them why they subscribed and
                  what you've been up to. 2-3 sentences max.
  Soft offer: Something free and immediately useful.
              "As a thank-you for sticking around, here's
              [resource/insight/tool] — no strings attached."
  CTA: Click to access the free resource OR reply to this email.

EMAIL 2: "Here's what you've missed" (Day 3)
SUBJECT LINE: "Your [Month] catch-up (3 things worth reading)"
CONTENT:
  - 3 best pieces of content, insights, or wins from the last 90 days
  - Make it scannable — short descriptions with clear links
  - Frame it as value, not guilt
  - CTA: Click through to any of the 3 links

EMAIL 3: "Something for you" (Day 7)
SUBJECT LINE: "A [discount/bonus/access] just for you"
CONTENT:
  - Exclusive offer for re-engaging — not available to the general list
  - This signals you value them specifically, not just their open rate
  - Options: Discount code, bonus content, free consultation,
             extended trial, early access to new feature
  - Hard deadline on the offer (5-7 days)
  - CTA: Claim your [offer]

EMAIL 4: "Should I stop emailing you?" (Day 14)
SUBJECT LINE OPTIONS:
  - "Should I stop emailing you?"
  - "Staying or going? (Takes 1 click)"
  - "I need to know one thing"
CONTENT:
  Opening: Blunt and honest. "I've sent you a few emails recently
            and haven't heard back. That's okay — inboxes get busy.
            But I want to respect your time."
  The choice:
    "If you'd like to keep getting [emails/content/offers from us],
     click YES — and I'll make sure what I send is worth your time.
     → [KEEP ME SUBSCRIBED — bright button]

     If you'd rather unsubscribe, you can do that below.
     No hard feelings."
  Automation: Anyone who does NOT click YES in 48 hours is
              automatically unsubscribed or moved to a suppression list.
```

### Step 3 — Design the Sunset Policy

Sunsetting is the disciplined practice of removing subscribers who will never re-engage. It feels counterintuitive (you are making your list smaller) but it is the right move for three reasons: it improves your sender score, it reduces ESP costs, and it makes your open rate metrics meaningful again.

**Sunset Decision Matrix:**

| Scenario | Action |
|----------|--------|
| Opened any email in win-back sequence | Move back to Warm segment. Reset engagement clock. |
| Clicked any link in win-back sequence | Move to Engaged segment. Tag interest area. |
| Clicked "Keep me subscribed" in final email | Move to Warm segment. Reduce frequency for 30 days. |
| Did not open ANY email in win-back sequence | Unsubscribe and move to suppression list. |
| Opened but never clicked over 6 months | Suppress from regular sends. Keep on announcement-only list. |
| Previously purchased but now cold | Move to customer win-back sequence (different approach — do not treat like never-bought subscribers) |

### Step 4 — Write Win-Back Subject Lines That Get Opened

Win-back subject lines have one job: pattern interrupt. The subscriber has been ignoring your emails. You need to look different from every other email in their inbox.

| Strategy | Example | Why It Works |
|----------|---------|--------------|
| **Direct acknowledgment** | "It's been 3 months, [Name]" | Honesty breaks through the noise |
| **Guilt-free curiosity** | "Did we do something wrong?" | Invites them to re-engage without accusation |
| **The blunt close** | "Should I stop emailing you?" | The threat of removal triggers action |
| **Unexpected value** | "A gift — no strings" | Pattern breaks the ignore-delete habit |
| **Personal reference** | "You downloaded [Lead Magnet] in August..." | Shows you remember them specifically |
| **Emoji as pattern interrupt** | "👋 Still interested in [Topic]?" | Visual break in text-heavy inbox |

**What NEVER works:** Subject lines that blame the subscriber, subject lines that are vague and could be from any brand, fake urgency ("THIS IS IMPORTANT!!!"), and lowercase manipulation tricks that have been overused ("hey").
