Thin content farms are being manually actioned. Sites that win are those with genuine topical depth, real author credentials, and brand signals that appear in the broader web (mentions, podcasts, industry citations). ## Workflow 1: Keyword Research and Clustering

### Step 1 — Seed Keyword Expansion

Start with 5-10 seed topics that represent your product or service. From each seed, expand in four directions:

**Informational modifiers** (top-of-funnel, high volume, low commercial value):
- "how to [seed]", "what is [seed]", "guide to [seed]", "[seed] tutorial", "[seed] for beginners"

**Comparative modifiers** (mid-funnel, medium volume, high commercial value):
- "[seed] vs [alternative]", "best [seed] tools", "[seed] alternatives", "[seed] pricing"

**Transactional modifiers** (bottom-funnel, lower volume, highest conversion value):
- "[seed] software", "buy [seed]", "[seed] agency", "[seed] service", "hire [seed]"

**Long-tail variants** (lower volume, very low competition, often converts well):
- "[seed] for [specific use case]", "[seed] [industry]", "how to [seed] without [pain point]"

**Data sources to pull from:**
- Google Search Console (queries you already get impressions for — low-hanging fruit)
- Ahrefs or Semrush keyword explorer (volume, difficulty, CPC)
- Google autocomplete and "People also ask" boxes (real user language)
- Reddit and Quora threads in your niche (how real people phrase questions)
- Competitor keyword gap analysis (keywords they rank for that you do not)

### Step 2 — Keyword Difficulty Triage

Apply this filter before clustering. Do not waste resources targeting keywords you cannot realistically rank for in the next 6-12 months.

| Keyword Difficulty | Domain Authority Required | Timeline to Rank | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| KD 0-20 | DA 10+ | 1-3 months | Target immediately — quick wins |
| KD 21-40 | DA 30+ | 3-6 months | Target if your DA qualifies |
| KD 41-60 | DA 50+ | 6-12 months | Target with strong content + links |
| KD 61-80 | DA 65+ | 12-24 months | Target only with dedicated link campaign |
| KD 81-100 | DA 80+ | 24+ months or never | Avoid unless you have major authority |

**Volume sanity check:** Do not obsess over volume. A keyword with 200 monthly searches in a B2B SaaS context may drive 3-5 qualified trials per month worth $50k ARR. A keyword with 50,000 searches in a consumer context may drive zero revenue. Always validate: what happens after someone searches this and lands on your site?

**Business value scoring:** Assign each keyword a business value score (1-5) based on how likely the searcher is to buy or convert. Prioritize keywords where (business value × monthly volume) is highest, not just volume alone.

### Step 3 — Cluster by Intent and Build the Topic Map

Group keywords into clusters where one pillar page targets the head term and multiple supporting pages target long-tail variants. A properly structured cluster has:

- **Pillar page** — targets the high-volume head keyword, covers the topic comprehensively (2,500-5,000 words), links to all supporting pages
- **Supporting pages** — each targets a specific long-tail variant or subtopic (1,000-2,000 words), links back to the pillar and to related supporting pages
- **Cluster size** — 1 pillar + 5-15 supporting pages is a healthy cluster; fewer than 5 supporting pages means you lack topical depth

```

======================

  Target keyword: [head term] — [volume] searches/mo, KD [X]
  URL: /[slug]
  Word count target: [2500-5000]

  Supporting Page 1
    Target keyword: [long-tail] — [volume] searches/mo, KD [X]
    URL: /[slug]
    Angle: [specific question this answers]

  Supporting Page 2
    Target keyword: [long-tail] — [volume] searches/mo, KD [X]
    URL: /[slug]

  - Pillar → all supporting pages (contextual links, not just a list)
```

## Workflow 2: Content Optimization

### Step 1 — SERP Analysis Before You Write a Word

Before optimizing or writing any page, spend 20 minutes analyzing the top 5-10 results for your target keyword. This is non-negotiable. The SERP tells you what Google currently believes is the best answer — your job is to produce something better by every meaningful measure.

**Questions to answer from the SERP:**
- What content format dominates? (long-form guides, list posts, tools/calculators, videos)
- What is the average word count of top-ranking pages? (match or exceed, not blindly — thin content that answers the question completely beats bloated content every time)
- What subheadings appear repeatedly across results? (required topics you must cover)
- What do the top results NOT cover? (your differentiation opportunity)
- Are there featured snippets? (if yes, what format — paragraph, list, table — and can you structure your content to win it)
- What is the reading level and tone? (match your audience's expectations)

### Step 2 — On-Page Optimization Checklist

**Title Tag:**
- 50-60 characters (longer truncates in SERPs, wasting click-through potential)
- Primary keyword as close to the front as possible without sounding robotic
- Include a modifier that signals value: "Complete Guide", "Step-by-Step", "[Year]", "Free Template"
- Do not stuff multiple keywords — write for click-through, not just crawlers

**Meta Description:**
- 150-160 characters (155 is the target)
- Include the primary keyword naturally
- Write it as ad copy: what will the reader get, and why should they click YOUR result over the 9 others?
- Include a soft CTA: "Learn how", "See examples", "Get the framework"

**URL Slug:**
- Short: 3-5 words maximum
- Contains the primary keyword
- No stop words (the, a, an, of) unless they are part of the natural keyword phrase
- Hyphens between words, never underscores

**Heading Structure:**
- H1 contains the primary keyword (one H1 per page, always)
- H2s cover the major topics and include secondary keywords where natural
- H3s break H2 sections into digestible sub-points
- Do not use headings for decoration — every H2 and H3 should represent a content section the user would want to navigate to

**Body Content:**
- Primary keyword in the first 100 words
- Secondary keywords distributed across H2/H3 headings and body text — use them where they fit naturally, never forced
- Minimum 2-3 internal links to related pages in your cluster (use keyword-rich anchor text, not "click here")
- 1-2 external links to authoritative sources (this signals confidence, not competition)
- At least one image, chart, or table with descriptive alt text containing the keyword
- FAQ section at the bottom targeting "People Also Ask" questions for your keyword

**Schema Markup:**
- Article schema for blog posts (author, date published, date modified)
- HowTo schema for tutorial content with step-by-step structure
- FAQ schema for pages with Q&A sections (directly increases PAA box visibility)
- Product schema for product pages
- Review schema when you have ratings or testimonials
- BreadcrumbList schema for all pages to show site hierarchy in SERPs

## Workflow 3: Technical SEO Audit

### Step 1 — Crawlability and Indexation

Before worrying about content or links, confirm Google can find and index your pages. A site with indexation problems will not rank no matter how good the content is.

**Core checks:**

**Robots.txt review:**
- Is robots.txt accessible at yourdomain.com/robots.txt?
- Are any important directories or file types accidentally blocked? (common mistake: blocking /wp-content/ which blocks image indexation)
- Is the sitemap URL declared in robots.txt?

**XML Sitemap:**
- Does a sitemap exist and is it submitted in Google Search Console?
- Does the sitemap only include pages you actually want indexed? (exclude paginated archives, tag pages, author pages, thin category pages unless they have value)
- Are all URLs in the sitemap returning 200 status codes? (sitemap with 301 or 404 URLs signals poor site hygiene)

**Indexation audit via Google Search Console:**
- Go to Index > Pages report: how many pages are "Not indexed" and why?
- Common culprits: "Duplicate, Google chose different canonical", "Crawled but not indexed", "Excluded by noindex tag"
- "Crawled but not indexed" is the most concerning — Google visited your page and decided it was not worth indexing. Usually means thin content or near-duplicate content.

**Coverage check:**
- Run `site:yourdomain.com` in Google to get a rough indexed page count
- Compare to total pages in your CMS — a large gap means indexation problems
- Check for www vs non-www and HTTP vs HTTPS canonicalization (there should be exactly one canonical version)

### Step 2 — Core Web Vitals and Page Speed

Core Web Vitals are a confirmed Google ranking factor. They matter most for competitive SERPs where content quality is roughly equivalent across results.

**The three CWV metrics and thresholds:**

| Metric | What It Measures | Good | Needs Improvement | Poor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LCP (Largest Contentful Paint) | How fast the main content loads | < 2.5s | 2.5s - 4.0s | > 4.0s |
| INP (Interaction to Next Paint) | How fast the page responds to user interaction | < 200ms | 200ms - 500ms | > 500ms |
| CLS (Cumulative Layout Shift) | How much content jumps around while loading | < 0.1 | 0.1 - 0.25 | > 0.25 |

**How to measure:**
- Google Search Console > Core Web Vitals report (real user data — the signal that actually affects rankings)
- PageSpeed Insights (pagespeed.web.dev) for page-level diagnostics
- Chrome DevTools > Lighthouse audit for development-time testing

**Most common LCP fixes:**
- Preload the hero image with `<link rel="preload" as="image" href="...">` 
- Serve images in next-gen formats (WebP, AVIF) — typically 25-35% smaller than JPEG
- Enable server-side caching and use a CDN (Cloudflare free tier solves this for most sites)
- Remove render-blocking scripts — defer or async all non-critical JavaScript

**Most common CLS fixes:**
- Always set explicit width and height on images and video embeds
- Reserve space for ads and embeds with CSS before they load
- Avoid inserting content above existing content after page load

## Workflow 4: Link Building Campaign

### Step 1 — Link Qualification Framework

Not all links are equal. A single link from a genuinely authoritative, relevant site in your niche is worth more than 50 links from generic directories. Before prospecting, define what a qualified link looks like for your site.

**Link qualification criteria:**

| Criterion | Minimum Threshold | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Domain Rating (Ahrefs DR) | DR 30+ preferred | DR < 20 is unlikely to move the needle |
| Organic traffic | 500+ visits/month | Links from dead sites pass no value |
| Topical relevance | Same or adjacent niche | Off-topic links are heavily discounted |
| Link placement | In-content, not footer/sidebar | Editorial placement is gold |
| Link type | Dofollow | Nofollow links have value for traffic and brand but not PageRank |
| Spam score (Moz) | < 5% | High spam score = likely penalized or low-quality domain |

**How many links do you need?** Pull the top 3 ranking pages for your target keyword in Ahrefs or Semrush. Look at their referring domain count. Your target is to match or exceed that number with links of equal or higher quality. This is the actual threshold — not an arbitrary goal.

### Step 2 — Link Acquisition Strategies by Type

**Strategy 1: Skyscraper Technique**
1. Find a piece of content in your niche that has many backlinks (Ahrefs > Site Explorer > search your topic)
2. Build a definitively better version — more comprehensive, more current, better designed, with original data
3. Find every site linking to the original via Ahrefs > Backlinks report
4. Email each linking site: "We noticed you linked to [article]. We built an updated version that covers [X, Y, Z that the original misses]. Thought you might want to know."

**Strategy 2: Digital PR / Original Data**
- Commission or run a survey (even 100-200 respondents via Typeform + Pollfish is enough)
- Compile an original industry report with statistics journalists need
- Create a tool or calculator (free tools earn passive links indefinitely)
- Pitch the data story to industry journalists — they need statistics and will link to the source
- Expected results: 20-100+ referring domains per successful PR piece

**Strategy 3: Guest Posting**
- Identify publications your target audience reads (search: "[niche] write for us" or "[niche] guest post guidelines")
- Qualify: DR 40+, real editorial standards, genuine organic traffic
- Pitch a specific angle with 3 headline options — make it easy for the editor to say yes
- Write a genuinely excellent article — do not treat it as a link vehicle disguised as content
- Place 1-2 contextual links to your site where they add real value

**Strategy 4: Broken Link Building**
1. Find competitor sites or resource pages in your niche
2. Use Ahrefs or Check My Links (Chrome extension) to find broken outbound links on those pages
3. Create (or point to existing) content that replaces the broken resource
4. Email the site owner: "Found a broken link on your page [X]. We have a resource that covers this topic well — happy to share if useful."

**Strategy 5: Unlinked Brand Mentions**
- Use Google Alerts or Ahrefs Alerts to monitor mentions of your brand or product
- When someone mentions you without linking, email them asking for a link
- This is the highest conversion outreach you will do — they already know and like you

## Workflow 5: Topical Authority Strategy

### Step 1 — Define the Topical Territory

Topical authority means Google recognizes your site as a comprehensive resource on a specific subject area. It is the difference between ranking for one keyword and ranking for an entire topic category with minimal link building.

**How Google assesses topical authority:**
- Does the site cover all major subtopics in the space? (breadth)
- Does the content demonstrate genuine expertise on each subtopic? (depth)
- Do other authoritative sites in the space link to this site? (external recognition)
- Are the content gaps smaller than competitors'? (comparative completeness)

**Choosing your territory:** Pick the narrowest topic space where you can realistically become the most comprehensive resource. A general blog about "marketing" will never achieve topical authority. A site about "email marketing for e-commerce brands" can.

Apply the "two-year test": could you publish 100 high-quality articles on this topic without repeating yourself? If yes, the territory is viable. If you hit a wall at 30 topics, the territory is too narrow.

### Step 2 — Content Hub Architecture

A topical authority site is structured as a hub-and-spoke model at scale:

```
TOPICAL AUTHORITY ARCHITECTURE
================================

TIER 1 — PILLAR PAGES (3-5 per site)
  These are your highest-traffic, highest-authority pages.
  Target: head keywords with 5,000-50,000+ monthly searches.
  Format: comprehensive guide, 3,000-5,000 words.
  Goal: rank for the head term AND serve as the hub for 10-20 supporting pages.

  Example: "Email Marketing Guide" — targets "email marketing"
    |
    +-- TIER 2 — CLUSTER PAGES (10-20 per pillar)
    |     Target: medium keywords with 500-5,000 monthly searches.
    |     Format: focused, 1,200-2,500 words.
    |     Goal: rank for long-tail and funnel PageRank up to the pillar.
    |
    |     Example: "Email Subject Line Best Practices"
    |     Example: "Email Segmentation Strategies"
    |     Example: "Email Automation Workflows"
    |
    +-- TIER 3 — SUPPORT PAGES (3-8 per cluster page)
          Target: long-tail keywords with 50-500 monthly searches.
          Format: ultra-specific, 600-1,200 words.
          Goal: capture very specific intent, build cluster depth.
    
          Example: "Welcome Email Subject Lines: 30 Examples"
          Example: "How to Segment an Email List by Purchase History"
```

**Publishing sequence matters:** Do not publish randomly. Publish the pillar page first (or update it first if it exists). Then publish cluster pages in batches of 3-5 to signal topical momentum. Google needs to see a pattern — one article about email marketing does not establish authority; 25 interconnected articles do.

### Step 3 — Topical Coverage Gap Analysis

Before writing new content, run a gap analysis to find what you are missing:

**Method 1: Keyword gap tool**
- In Ahrefs or Semrush, run a Content Gap or Keyword Gap analysis
- Input your domain + 3-5 competitor domains
- Filter for keywords where competitors rank in top 20 but you rank outside top 100
- These are your coverage gaps — topics the search ecosystem expects a site in your space to cover

**Method 2: Manual SERP audit**
- Search your core topic in Google: "site:competitor.com" to see all their indexed pages
- Review their blog/resource section directly
- Note every subtopic they cover that you do not
- Build a gap list and prioritize by keyword volume + business value

**Topical authority build schedule:**

| Month | Activity | Target Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | Audit existing content, map current coverage, identify gaps | Gap analysis document, prioritized content list |
| 3-4 | Publish pillar pages (or update existing) | 2-3 pillar pages at 3,000+ words |
| 5-8 | Publish cluster pages in batches | 15-25 cluster pages, 3-5 per week |
| 9-12 | Publish tier 3 support pages, internal link audit | 20-40 support pages, full internal link map |
| 13+ | Maintain and refresh (update older content annually) | Ongoing content quality signals |

## Workflow 6: SERP Feature Targeting

### Step 1 — Identify Which SERP Features to Target

Not every query triggers special features, and not every feature is worth targeting. Start by identifying which features appear for your target keywords and whether they drive clicks.

**SERP Feature Inventory:**

| Feature | Triggered By | Click Value | How to Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Featured Snippet (Paragraph) | Informational "what is", "how does" queries | High — position 0, appears above organic results | Answer the question in 40-60 words in a single paragraph under a heading that mirrors the query |
| Featured Snippet (List) | "How to", "steps to", "ways to" queries | High | Use numbered lists (for sequential steps) or bulleted lists (for non-sequential items) |
| Featured Snippet (Table) | Comparison queries, "best X for Y" | Medium | Include a properly formatted HTML table with the compared data |
| People Also Ask | Almost any informational query | Medium — expands your real estate on the page | Add FAQ sections targeting PAA questions with concise 40-60 word answers |
| Knowledge Panel | Brand/entity queries | Brand protection | Claim and verify Google Business Profile; build consistent NAP; get Wikipedia or Wikidata entry |
| Local Pack | "[service] near me", "[service] [city]" queries | Very high for local businesses | Optimize Google Business Profile, build local citations, earn local reviews |
| Image Pack | Visual topics (recipes, products, how-to) | Medium for visual niches | Optimize image file names, alt text, captions; use structured data |
| Sitelinks Search Box | Brand name queries | Brand experience signal | Implement Sitelinks Searchbox schema; requires high brand search volume |

### Step 2 — Featured Snippet Capture Framework

Featured snippets are won by formatting, not just content quality. Google pulls the snippet from the page that best answers the query in the specific format the query demands.

**Paragraph snippet formula:**
```
[Heading that mirrors the target query]

[Topic] is [direct definition or answer]. [One sentence of elaboration that adds 
necessary context]. [Optional: one sentence with the most important implication or caveat].
```
Total: 40-60 words. Never more than 60 — longer answers get truncated or passed over.

**List snippet formula:**
```
[Heading: "How to [Task]" or "[Number] Ways to [Goal]"]

1. [Step name]: [One sentence description]
2. [Step name]: [One sentence description]
3. [Step name]: [One sentence description]
...
```
Use `<ol>` tags for steps (ordered matters), `<ul>` for items where order does not matter.

**Table snippet formula:**
```html
<table>
  <thead>
    <tr><th>[Column 1]</th><th>[Column 2]</th><th>[Column 3]</th></tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>[Value]</td><td>[Value]</td><td>[Value]</td></tr>
    ...
  </tbody>
</table>
```
Google pulls tables directly from HTML. Use real `<table>` markup, not CSS-styled divs pretending to be tables.

**Snippet-targeting checklist:**
- [ ] Identify which queries in GSC trigger featured snippets where you rank in positions 2-10 (these are your steal opportunities)
- [ ] Add a heading that exactly matches the query phrasing to the relevant section of your page
- [ ] Write the answer in the correct format (paragraph, list, or table)
- [ ] Keep paragraph answers to 40-60 words
- [ ] Place the formatted answer immediately below the heading (do not bury it after preamble)
- [ ] Include the query keyword in the heading and in the answer

### Step 3 — People Also Ask Domination

PAA boxes expand the search results page. Appearing in PAA alongside your organic ranking effectively doubles your page 1 presence. More importantly, PAA questions often represent the next query in the searcher's journey — answering them keeps users in your content ecosystem.

**How to find PAA questions to target:**
1. Search your target keyword in Google — note all PAA questions that appear
2. Click each PAA to expand it — this triggers additional related questions to appear, giving you a deeper question tree
3. Use tools like AlsoAsked.com or AnswerThePublic to map the full PAA tree for a topic
4. Cross-reference with your keyword research — which PAA questions have standalone search volume worth targeting?

**PAA implementation:**

Add an FAQ section at the bottom of every pillar page and major cluster page. Use FAQ schema markup:

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "FAQPage",
  "mainEntity": [
    {
      "@type": "Question",
      "name": "What is [question exactly as it appears in PAA]?",
      "acceptedAnswer": {
        "@type": "Answer",
        "text": "[40-60 word answer. Direct, complete, no filler.]"
      }
    }
  ]
}
```

**Local Pack optimization (for local businesses):**

The local pack is the 3-business map cluster that appears for local intent queries. Ranking here is often more valuable than ranking #1 in organic results — the pack appears above organic results and includes star ratings, phone numbers, and directions.

Local pack ranking factors in order of impact:
1. **Google Business Profile completeness and activity** — fully complete every field; post weekly; respond to every review within 24 hours; add photos monthly
2. **Review velocity and rating** — more reviews, higher rating, consistent new reviews. Target: 50+ reviews, 4.5+ average. Get 2-4 new reviews per month minimum.
3. **NAP consistency** — your business Name, Address, and Phone number must be identical across your website, GBP, Yelp, Facebook, Apple Maps, and all directories
4. **Proximity** — Google prioritizes businesses physically close to the searcher. You cannot change your address, but you can open locations or service area listings.
5. **Local citations** — listings on authoritative local directories (Yelp, BBB, Chamber of Commerce, industry-specific directories) that match your NAP exactly
