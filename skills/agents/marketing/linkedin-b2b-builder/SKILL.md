## Workflow 1: Build a Thought Leadership Content Strategy

### Step 1 — Define Your Content Pillars

Content pillars are the 3–5 topics you will own on LinkedIn. Every post lives inside a pillar. Every pillar connects back to your business.

**The B2B Pillar Framework:**

| Pillar Type | Purpose | Examples |
|-------------|---------|---------|
| **Expert insights** | Establish authority in your category | "5 things I learned closing 200 enterprise deals," frameworks, counterintuitive lessons |
| **Personal story** | Build trust and connection | Career pivots, failures, what changed your thinking, behind-the-scenes decisions |
| **Industry commentary** | Show you have a POV | Reactions to trends, what the industry gets wrong, predictions |
| **Company/product** | Drive pipeline (use sparingly, max 1 in 5 posts) | Case studies, customer wins, product updates framed as lessons |
| **Engagement bait** | Expand reach through replies | Polls, "hot take" questions, controversial opinions with an ask to weigh in |

**Pillar Selection Rule:** Your pillars should answer: "Why would my ideal buyer follow me for 12 months?" If the pillar would not hold their attention for a year, replace it.

### Step 2 — Content Format Mix

LinkedIn's algorithm treats different formats very differently. Use this mix to maximize both reach and depth.

| Format | Avg. Engagement Rate | Reach | Best For | Post Frequency |
|--------|---------------------|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Text post (short)** | 3–5% | High | Hot takes, one-line insights, reactions | 3x/week |
| **Text post (storytelling)** | 4–8% | Very High | Personal stories, lessons, frameworks | 2x/week |
| **Carousel (PDF)** | 2–4%, high saves | Moderate | Step-by-step guides, data, frameworks | 1x/week |
| **Poll** | 8–15% | Very High | Market research, opinion gathering, engagement | 1–2x/month |
| **Video (native)** | 3–6% | High | Behind-the-scenes, short lessons, demos | 1x/month |
| **Newsletter issue** | N/A (email) | Persistent | Deep dives, long-form thought leadership | Bi-weekly |

**The golden rule:** Text posts with storytelling structures consistently outperform all other formats. The algorithm rewards dwell time (people re-reading) and comments (substantive conversation). A 300-word personal story with a business lesson will beat a 10-slide carousel every single week.

### Step 3 — Posting Cadence

| Account Stage | Posts/Week | Best Days | Best Times |
|---------------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Starting (0–500 connections) | 3 | Tue, Wed, Thu | 7–9 AM, 12 PM |
| Growing (500–5K followers) | 4–5 | Mon–Thu | 7–9 AM, 12–1 PM |
| Established (5K–20K followers) | 3–5 | Mon–Fri | 7–9 AM |
| Authority (20K+ followers) | 3–5 | Mon–Thu | 7–8 AM |

**What kills reach on LinkedIn:**
- Links in the post body (paste them in the first comment instead)
- Tagging people who will not engage (looks like engagement baiting)
- Posting and disappearing — the first 60 minutes of engagement determine your reach ceiling
- Company page-only content without personal amplification

### Step 4 — The 90-Day Content Calendar System

**Week-by-week template:**

```
WEEK STRUCTURE:
Mon: Insight post (framework, lesson, industry take)
Tue: Story post (personal experience with business takeaway)
Wed: Engagement post (poll, question, or hot take)
Thu: Proof post (case study, customer result, behind-the-scenes)
Fri: Optional — curated insight or light engagement (question to audience)

MONTHLY STRUCTURE:
Week 1: Focus on Pillar 1
Week 2: Focus on Pillar 2
Week 3: Focus on Pillar 3
Week 4: Cross-pillar week + newsletter issue
```

**Content ideation sources:**
- Customer questions from sales calls (your ICP is telling you what to post about)
- Industry news you have a strong opinion on
- Lessons from your last 30 days of work
- Mistakes you made and what you learned
- Results you achieved with specific numbers

## Workflow 2: Write High-Performing LinkedIn Posts

### The Most Important Rule: The Hook

LinkedIn truncates every post at approximately 3 lines (roughly 210–240 characters) with a "...see more" cutoff. If your first line does not earn the click, no one reads anything else. The hook is not the intro — the hook is the entire marketing budget for your post.

### Hook Formulas That Work

```
THE SPECIFIC NUMBER:
"I sent 1,247 cold LinkedIn messages last year. Here's what a 38% reply rate looks like:"

THE COUNTERINTUITIVE:
"Posting every day killed my LinkedIn reach. Here's what I did instead:"

THE CONFESSION:
"I almost missed our Series A because of a LinkedIn mistake most founders make."

THE PATTERN INTERRUPT:
"LinkedIn advice you should ignore: [common but wrong tactic]"

THE STORY OPENER:
"In 2021 I had 300 LinkedIn followers and zero inbound leads. Last month: 47K followers, 31 inbound demos."

THE BOLD CLAIM:
"Your company page is costing you leads. Here's the data:"

THE QUESTION HOOK:
"What do the top 1% of B2B LinkedIn accounts all have in common? I analyzed 200 of them."

THE TIMEFRAME:
"18 months ago I quit running LinkedIn ads. We grew revenue 3x. Thread:"
```

**Hook writing rules:**
- One sentence, never two
- Specific numbers beat vague claims every time ("3x" not "significantly")
- Create a gap — tease what comes next without giving it away
- Never start with "I am" or "We are" — nobody cares about your identity, they care about your insight
- Never start with a company name or product feature

### Post Structures That Work

**The Storytelling Structure (highest engagement):**
```
LINE 1 (Hook): [Compelling first line that opens a loop]

LINE 2-3: [Context — set the scene, make it specific]

LINE 4-6: [The conflict or turning point]

LINE 7-9: [The resolution or lesson]

LINE 10-11: [The business takeaway or framework extracted from the story]

LINE 12 (CTA): [Single soft ask — comment, DM, or follow]
```

**The Framework/List Structure (highest saves):**
```
LINE 1 (Hook): "[N] things that [outcome] — most people only know #[X]:"

LINE 2-3: [Brief context on why this matters]

LINE 4+: 
→ [Point 1 with one sentence of context]
→ [Point 2 with one sentence of context]
→ [Point 3 with one sentence of context]
...

LAST LINE: [Strong close — either the "most important" point or a CTA]
```

**The Contrarian Structure (highest reach via comments):**
```
LINE 1 (Hook): "Hot take: [industry belief everyone holds] is wrong."

LINE 2-3: [What everyone does and why it makes sense on the surface]

LINE 4-6: [What you believe instead and why]

LINE 7-8: [Evidence or example from your experience]

LINE 9: [Invitation to disagree — "Change my mind." or "Curious if others see this differently."]
```

### Engagement Triggers

| Trigger | How to Use | Example |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| **Ask for agreement** | End post with "Agree or disagree?" | Drives comments from both sides |
| **Invite experience** | "What's been your experience with [topic]?" | Easy for anyone to answer |
| **Ask for the vote** | "Which would you choose? Option A or B?" | Low-barrier engagement |
| **Make them disagree** | Take a clear stance that some will push back on | Controversy drives comments |
| **Tell them what to DM** | "DM me [word] and I'll send you [resource]" | Drives DMs for lead gen |
| **Tag prompt** | "Tag someone who needs to see this" | Expands reach to new audiences |

### Formatting Rules

- One idea per post — never two
- White space is not optional — break every 2–3 lines
- No bullet points in the first 3 lines (pushes the "see more" cutoff earlier)
- No links in the post body — drop them in the first comment
- Hashtags: 3 maximum, placed at the end or in first comment
- Emojis: use sparingly as bullets (→, ✓) or none at all — never decorative rows of emojis
- End with a single CTA — asking for multiple actions kills conversion

**STOPPING POINT 3 — Post Writing**

1. **Write 5 posts ready to publish** — Full posts across different structures and pillars
2. **Write 10 hook variations for one topic** — Different angles on the same core idea
3. **Rewrite an underperforming post** — Diagnose what went wrong and rebuild it
4. **Build a post template library** — 5 reusable templates for each content pillar
5. **Create a 30-day post calendar** — Every post pre-written, formatted, and ready to schedule

## Workflow 3: Optimize Your LinkedIn Profile for Inbound

### The Profile as a Landing Page

Your LinkedIn profile is visited by every person who reads your post, accepts your connection, or finds you in search. Most profiles fail at all three jobs: they do not rank in search (no keywords), they do not convert visitors to followers (no compelling story), and they do not generate leads (no clear next step). This workflow fixes all three.

### Step 1 — Headline Formula

The headline is the most keyword-dense real estate on LinkedIn and appears everywhere: search results, post attribution, connection requests, and comments.

**Headline Formula:**
```
[Role/What You Do] | [Who You Help] | [Result You Deliver] | [Proof or Differentiator]
```

**Examples:**
```
CEO at [Company] | Helping B2B SaaS founders close enterprise deals | $0 to $5M ARR playbook | DM me "SCALE"

Fractional CMO | B2B demand gen for Series A–C companies | 3x pipeline in 90 days or we find out why | 200+ campaigns built

Head of Sales | Teaching SaaS AEs to close without discounting | Author of [Newsletter] | 10K+ subscribers

Founder @ [Company] | We cut SaaS spend by 40% for mid-market teams | Used by [notable customer logos] | Try free ↓
```

**What to avoid:**
- "Experienced professional passionate about innovation" (meaningless)
- Just your job title with no value proposition
- Company name as the headline (Google indexes your name + company already)

### Step 2 — About Section (The Sales Page)

The About section is the one place on LinkedIn where long-form storytelling is expected. Most people write a bio. Write a sales page.

**About Section Structure:**
```
PARAGRAPH 1 — The Hook (2–3 sentences):
Open with the most compelling version of your story or the biggest result you have delivered.
"In 2019 I took a company from $400K to $4M ARR in 18 months using nothing but LinkedIn outreach and founder-led content. Since then I have helped 60+ B2B founders build the same system."

PARAGRAPH 2 — Who You Help and How:
"I work with [ICP] who struggle with [pain]. Most of them have tried [common approach]. What actually works is [your methodology]."

PARAGRAPH 3 — Proof and Credibility:
Specific numbers, notable clients (if permissible), media features, book/newsletter stats.

PARAGRAPH 4 — What You Offer:
Clear description of services/products with one CTA.
"If you want [result], DM me [word] or visit [URL]."

PARAGRAPH 5 — Personal (optional but effective):
One sentence of humanity. Where you are based, what you geek out on outside of work. Makes you a person, not a profile.
```

### Step 3 — Featured Section (The Conversion Layer)

The Featured section sits above your experience and is prime real estate for inbound conversion. Use it as a mini storefront.

**Featured Section Priority Order:**
1. Lead magnet or free resource (newsletter signup, checklist, template)
2. Best-performing post or LinkedIn article (social proof of authority)
3. Case study or customer story (conversion evidence)
4. Product or service page (for bottom-of-funnel visitors)
5. Media feature or press mention (credibility signal)

**Featured section rules:**
- Write custom titles for each item — "Download the Free B2B LinkedIn Playbook" beats the auto-generated post title
- Limit to 3–4 items — more is not better; it creates decision paralysis
- Review and refresh quarterly — stale featured content signals an inactive profile

### Step 4 — Social Proof Stack

| Element | What It Signals | How to Optimize |
|---------|----------------|----------------|
| **Profile photo** | Trustworthiness, approachability | Professional headshot, plain background, smile, 400x400px minimum |
| **Banner image** | Brand, positioning, CTA | Include tagline + CTA ("DM me for [resource]") |
| **Connection count** | Network credibility | 500+ shows as "500+" — a visible threshold worth reaching |
| **Recommendations** | Peer validation | Request 3–5 specific recommendations with context ("mention the [campaign] we ran") |
| **Skills endorsements** | Search ranking | Pin your top 3 skills — keyword-match to what buyers search |
| **Experience section** | Career proof | Lead with impact ("Grew ARR from $X to $Y") not job descriptions |

**STOPPING POINT 4 — Profile Optimization**

1. **Write the complete optimized profile** — Headline, about section, featured section plan, banner brief
2. **Optimize for search ranking** — Keyword research for your ICP's search terms, placement strategy
3. **Build the recommendations strategy** — Who to ask, what to brief them on, example request message
4. **Redesign the featured section** — What to include, custom titles, conversion path
5. **Profile audit** — Review an existing profile against this framework, list every change to make

## Workflow 4: Run a Connection + Outreach Campaign

### The LinkedIn Outreach Playbook

LinkedIn cold outreach works at 2–5x the reply rate of cold email when done correctly — because it comes with a face, a history of content, and a social context that email cannot replicate. The trap: most people treat it like cold email. Spray-and-pray connection requests with immediate pitches train LinkedIn to suppress your account and train prospects to ignore your name.

The playbook: build the relationship before the ask. Give value before you take it.

### Step 1 — Prospect Targeting

**Using LinkedIn's free search + filters:**
- Title + keyword filters: "VP Sales" + "SaaS" + "51–200 employees"
- Geography: target one city or region first to build local proof
- Mutual connections: 2nd-degree connections accept at 40–60% vs 20–30% for cold

**Using Sales Navigator (recommended at $99/month for serious outreach):**
```
ACCOUNT FILTERS:
- Industry: [Your target verticals]
- Headcount: [Your ICP range]
- Revenue (if available): [Your ICP range]
- Technologies used: [Relevant stack if applicable]

LEAD FILTERS:
- Job title / seniority
- Years in current role (1–2 years = most likely to make changes)
- Posted on LinkedIn in last 30 days (active = more likely to reply)
- Following your company page (warmest possible lead)
```

**Prospect scoring:**
- Warm (5 stars): Follows your content, commented on a post, mutual connection, referral
- Qualified (3 stars): Matches ICP criteria, title + company size + industry
- Cold (1 star): Right title, wrong context — no mutual signals

### Step 2 — Connection Request Sequence

**Connection request (300 characters max — no sales pitch):**

```
OPTION A (Content-based):
"[First name] — your comment on [topic/person's post] resonated with me. We're both [doing similar thing/facing similar challenge]. Connecting."

OPTION B (Mutual connection):
"[First name] — [Mutual name] mentioned you. I work in [their space] and think we'd have good things to talk about."

OPTION C (Their content):
"[First name] — I saw your post about [topic]. Exactly what I've been thinking. Connecting."

OPTION D (Simple + specific):
"[First name] — I work with [ICP descriptor] and your profile stood out. Connecting to share ideas."
```

**Connection acceptance rates by approach:**

| Approach | Acceptance Rate | Notes |
|---------|----------------|-------|
| Blank request (no note) | 30–40% | Fine for warm leads; risky for cold |
| Generic "Let's connect" | 20–30% | No context = no reason to accept |
| Specific, no pitch | 40–60% | Best practice — references something real |
| Immediate pitch in request | 10–20% | Triggers spam reflex; avoid |
| Referral mention | 55–70% | Highest acceptance — name-drop when true |

### Step 3 — The Follow-Up Sequence

After connection accepted, run this 4-message sequence over 14 days. Rule: never pitch before message 3.

```
MESSAGE 1 (Day 1 — Thank you + value):
"[First name] — thanks for connecting.

I've been following what [their company] is doing with [topic] — [specific observation that shows you've done homework, 1 sentence].

I work with [ICP descriptor] to [result you deliver]. Not pitching — just noticed the overlap.

Wanted to share this: [link to genuinely useful resource, case study, or your post on a topic they'd care about]. Thought it might be useful given what you're working on."

MESSAGE 2 (Day 5 — Insight, no ask):
"[First name] — one more thing, since you're in [space]:

[3–4 sentence insight, framework, or lesson from your work that is directly relevant to their role. Specific, not generic.]

Working on anything like this at [company]?"

MESSAGE 3 (Day 10 — The soft ask):
"[First name] — [refer to their response to message 2, or to something from their recent posts/activity].

I've helped [2–3 companies like theirs] [specific result with number]. 

Worth 20 minutes to see if there's a fit? Happy to share exactly how we did it — no deck, just a conversation."

MESSAGE 4 (Day 14 — The break-up):
"[First name] — last note from me.

If [problem you solve] is not a priority right now, completely understand. Happy to reconnect when the timing makes sense.

If there is any chance a 20-minute call could be useful, just say yes and I'll send a calendar link."
```

**Sequence performance benchmarks:**

| Message | Open Rate | Reply Rate |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| Message 1 (value) | 80–90% | 15–25% |
| Message 2 (insight) | 70–80% | 10–20% |
| Message 3 (soft ask) | 60–75% | 8–15% |
| Message 4 (break-up) | 55–70% | 5–12% |

### Step 4 — Volume and Pipeline Math

**Conservative scenario (3 hours/week of outreach):**
```
50 connection requests/week
× 40% acceptance rate
= 20 new connections/week

20 new connections × 20% reply rate to message 1
= 4 conversations/week

4 conversations × 25% call booking rate
= 1 call/week

1 call/week × 20% close rate (for qualified calls)
= ~1 new customer/5 weeks from LinkedIn alone
```

**STOPPING POINT 5 — Outreach Campaign**

1. **Build the complete outreach campaign** — Targeting criteria, connection requests, full 4-message sequence
2. **Write the personalized message templates** — 5 variations for each message based on ICP segment
3. **Set up the prospecting system** — Filters, daily workflow, CRM tracking
4. **Create the objection response library** — How to handle "not interested," "send me a deck," "wrong timing"
5. **Run the pipeline math for your goal** — Reverse-engineer from revenue target to daily outreach volume

## Workflow 5: Launch a LinkedIn Newsletter

### Why LinkedIn Newsletter Beats Substack for B2B

LinkedIn Newsletter is the only email list you can build with zero acquisition cost — subscribers come from your existing followers, and LinkedIn sends them an email when you publish. Each new subscriber is a warm follower who already trusts you. Typical B2B newsletters on LinkedIn see 40–55% open rates vs 25–30% on Substack for equivalent audiences.

The trade-off: you do not own the list. Use LinkedIn Newsletter to build audience, then migrate the most engaged readers to your own email list over time.

### Step 1 — Newsletter Positioning

Before you write a single issue, define:

```
NAME: [Specific, searchable, conveys the benefit — not "The [Your Name] Newsletter"]
TAGLINE: [One sentence: who it is for + what they will get + how often]
CADENCE: [Weekly | Bi-weekly | Monthly — bi-weekly is the B2B sweet spot]
FORMAT: [What every issue contains — readers like knowing what to expect]
```

**Newsletter name formulas:**
```
THE OUTCOME: "The Pipeline Letter — B2B growth strategies that close deals"
THE NICHE: "[Industry] Insider — what [role] need to know this week"
THE CONTRARIAN: "Wrong by Default — why the common playbook fails and what actually works"
THE NUMBER: "3-2-1 [Topic] — 3 insights, 2 tactics, 1 question every [cadence]"
```

### Step 2 — The Issue Structure (Every Time)

Readers stay subscribed because of predictability. Surprise them with content, not format.

**B2B Newsletter Issue Template:**
```
SUBJECT LINE (treat like an email subject — it determines open rate):
"[Specific insight or question] + [proof or number]"
Examples:
- "Why we stopped doing discovery calls (and booked 40% more meetings)"
- "The outreach sequence that gets 38% reply rates"
- "What I got wrong about content marketing for 3 years"

OPENING (2–3 sentences):
Connect to something timely (a trend, a news item, a recent conversation with a customer) to signal the issue is current and relevant.

THE MAIN PIECE (400–800 words):
One big idea, thoroughly explored. Framework, case study, or contrarian take. Include:
- Specific numbers wherever possible
- A concrete example or story
- An actionable takeaway the reader can use this week

THE QUICK WIN (optional, 100–200 words):
One tactic, tool, or template they can apply immediately. Low-friction, high-perceived value.

CLOSING CTA (2–3 sentences):
One ask. Options: reply with their take, DM you for a resource, share with someone it would help, visit your product page (use sparingly).
```

### Step 3 — Launch Strategy (First 30 Days)

**Pre-launch (Week -1):**
```
POST 1: "I'm launching a newsletter next week. Here's what it's about and who it's for: [2–3 sentences]
Subscribe here: [LinkedIn newsletter link]
First issue drops [date]."

POST 2 (2 days later): "Why I'm starting [Newsletter Name] — and what you will get from it every [cadence]:
[3 bullet points of specific value]
Link in first comment."
```

**Launch week:**
- Publish Issue 1 and post about it with a pull-quote from inside
- DM your top 50 connections personally: "I published the first issue of [name]. Thought of you specifically because [1 sentence]. Would love your take."
- Respond to every comment on the launch post within 2 hours

**Subscriber growth benchmarks:**
| Milestone | Typical Timeline | Driver |
|-----------|----------------|--------|
| 100 subscribers | Week 1 | Launch announcement posts |
| 500 subscribers | Month 1–2 | Consistent posting + CTAs |
| 1,000 subscribers | Month 2–4 | Viral post + newsletter mention |
| 5,000 subscribers | Month 6–12 | Compound growth from quality issues |
| 10,000+ subscribers | Year 1–2 | Cross-promotions, media mentions |

### Step 4 — Growing Subscribers After Launch

- Add newsletter link to your profile headline with a CTA ("↓ Subscribe: [Newsletter Name]")
- Add to featured section as first item
- Mention it at the end of 1 in every 4 posts (not every post — it feels spammy)
- Collaborate with 2–3 other newsletter writers in adjacent niches for cross-promotion
- Repurpose the best insight from each issue as a standalone post — link to full issue in comments

**STOPPING POINT 6 — Newsletter Launch**

1. **Build the complete newsletter launch plan** — Name, positioning, first 4 issue outlines, 30-day launch calendar
2. **Write Issue 1** — Full first issue using the template, subject line, and CTA
3. **Write 3 launch posts** — Pre-launch teaser, launch day announcement, and one-week follow-up
4. **Create the subscriber growth system** — Profile CTAs, post cadence, DM outreach plan
5. **Design the editorial calendar** — 12 issue topics planned out with format variation

## Workflow 6: Use LinkedIn for Lead Generation

### The B2B LinkedIn Lead Gen Ecosystem

LinkedIn has four distinct lead generation channels, and the companies winning are using all four in a coordinated system rather than betting on any single one.

```
CHANNEL 1: Inbound from content
(Post → comment → profile visit → DM or form fill)

CHANNEL 2: Outbound via connections + DMs
(Prospecting → connection → sequence → call)

CHANNEL 3: LinkedIn Lead Gen Forms
(Content ad or InMail → native form → CRM sync)

CHANNEL 4: Events + webinars
(LinkedIn event → registration → follow-up sequence)
```

### Step 1 — The Content-to-DM Pipeline

The highest-quality leads on LinkedIn come from people who find you through your content. These leads have pre-qualified themselves: they read your post, agreed with your take, and chose to reach out. Close rates on inbound LinkedIn DMs are 3–5x higher than outbound cold sequences.

**How to engineer inbound DMs from content:**

```
TACTIC 1 — The DM trigger post:
"I just finished a guide to [topic]. If you want a copy, DM me the word '[keyword]' and I'll send it over."
Result: Creates a pipeline of people who self-identify as interested in your topic.

TACTIC 2 — The comment-to-DM conversion:
After someone comments substantively on your post, reply to their comment and send a DM:
"[Name] — your comment about [specific point] was excellent. I replied to it above, but wanted to reach out directly too — we are doing interesting work in [area] and your POV would be valuable. Mind if I share a bit more context?"

TACTIC 3 — The poll-to-DM pipeline:
Post a poll on a pain point your ICP faces.
After it closes, DM everyone who voted for the most painful option:
"[Name] — I saw you voted [option] in my poll about [topic]. That's the most common pain point we hear from [ICP]. I had a few thoughts I wanted to share — mind if I send them?"
```

### Step 2 — Sales Navigator Prospecting System

**Daily prospecting workflow (45 minutes):**

```
STEP 1 (10 min): Build or refresh a saved search with ICP filters
STEP 2 (10 min): Filter for "Posted on LinkedIn in last 30 days" — active users only
STEP 3 (10 min): View profiles of top 20 prospects, note any recent posts or activity
STEP 4 (15 min): Send personalized connection requests referencing their content
```

**Sales Navigator alerts to monitor:**
- Job change alerts: Executives who just joined a new company are actively building their team and vendor stack — warmest possible timing
- Company growth alerts: Companies that just raised funding or announced headcount growth have budget
- Content alerts: Prospects who posted recently are active and more likely to reply

### Step 3 — LinkedIn InMail Strategy

InMail has a 10–25% reply rate when done correctly vs 1–5% for cold email. You pay for InMail credits — do not waste them on generic messages.

**InMail template that works:**
```
SUBJECT: [Specific observation about their business, not "Quick question"]
Examples:
- "Your [recent post / content / strategy] — one addition"
- "[Mutual connection] suggested I reach out"
- "Re: [specific pain point relevant to their role]"

BODY:
"[First name] —
```
