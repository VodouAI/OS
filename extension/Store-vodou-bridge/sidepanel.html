Exactly what does it read?
Vodou can show what you already know about the page in front of you — notes you made while you were on it, and anything from the same site.

To do that, this panel reads the address and title of the tab you are viewing and sends them to Vodou on your own computer. It happens only while this panel is open. Pages you merely visit are never recorded, and your browsing history is never collected, stored or sent anywhere.

It can also suggest memories related to what you're typing: when you pause while writing in a text box on the page, what you've typed so far goes to Vodou on your computer to find related memories. Never in password, payment or one-time-code fields; nothing you type is stored by the extension.

And when you ask — right-click → Fill this form from Vodou — it reads that page's form field labels (never password, payment or one-time-code fields, never what's already typed) and proposes answers from your memory for you to review. Only the answers you tick are written; the form is never submitted.

On other sites you can also choose Enable Vodou on this site: Chrome asks once for that site, and Vodou's page script then runs there automatically. Nothing is enabled until you choose it, and you can turn any site off again.
